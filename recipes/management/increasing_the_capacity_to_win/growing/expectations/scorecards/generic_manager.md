# Background
This is a generic scorecard that represents *one core* of a Manager's job. It doesn't even have to apply to Engineering Management; it's a good starting point for any company or role-specific Manager scorecard.

Note: like any generic scorecard, some of these may not apply to you. 

# People Manager Scorecard
A Manager's job is to *Win, and to Increase the Capacity to Win*. (put link here) There are about a bazillion things a Manager can do to accomplish these two things; the scope is beyond this scorecard. For the purpose here, we have 6 areas, 3 for Winning and 3 for Increasing the Capacity. These are not the only ones that matter; I just chose them for demonstration purposes.

We will grade every sub-bullet on a 1-5 scale:

1. Cannot do this now, and unlikely to learn in <6 months.
2. Learning and should be adequate in <3 months.
3. Doing this well. 
4. Very strong in this behavior.
5. Role model for the team and organization.

(note: it's impossible for one person to be a 5 on all of these. That is not the expectation. Instead, find the 3 behaviors you should be a '5' at and improve those. Also find the 3 behaviors you CANNOT be a 1 at without getting fired, and fix those) 


1. Aims Straight: Makes "Winning" Clear
    1. Creates a clear understanding of the goals for themselves, refreshed every week. Defines success in customer and business outcomes whenever possible. Personally commits to hitting the goals.
    2. Clearly communicates the goals to the team and stakeholders. Validates that the relevant people also know the goals and are committed to hitting them. Doesn't take silence for a "yes". 
    3. Doesn't pursue goals that they don't understand -- or don't matter. They are allergic to wasting the team's time. They chase down expectations and question assumptions until they can own the 'why?'.
    4. Avoids unrealistic goals. Iterates on last week's delivery to set reasonable outcomes this week. Works with the team to ensure their understanding of 'capacity' is correct. Delineates 'stretch' vs 'must-hit'. 
    5. Flexible in updating goals when the plates shift BUT holds the team and themselves accountable when not delivering, without excuses. The team knows if it is winning -- or not.
2. Trims Aggressively: Delivers Value Sooner
    1. Always on the lookout for clever ways to de-scope deliverables to get 'just enough'. Understands customers' needs well enough to spot 'not really needed' "requirements". Negotiates hard.
    2. Always on the lookout for their team overcomplicating the approach. Challenges 'clever' ideas that are too complex. Errs on the side of "Keep it Simple, Stupid".
    3. Defaults to 'ship & iterate' mentality - because the best way to cut is to realize it isn't actually needed.
    4. Uses experiments, prototypes, demos, existing tools, etc to reach the goal faster. Has their own 'clever' ideas that actually do deliver 80% of the value in 20% of the effort.
    5. Understands the 'untrimmable' (e.g. reliability, UX, whatever matters) and never accidentally trims the wrong things.
3. Motivates Effectively: Gets the Maximum out of People
    1. Connects the work to the *real* value: real people, real problems, real $, etc. Makes it concrete by connecting the people directly to the source: talking to customers, visiting sites, watching the final presentations, etc.
    2. Explains the stakes of messing up honestly, including customer, revenue, or company impact, without creating unnecessary crises. 
    3. Understands what motivates *individual* members on their team and connects those motivations back to the work (where possible). 
    4. Celebrates wins, big and small, in a natural way that resonates with the team. They all feel appreciated and valuable.  Uses various tools (1:1s, feedback, team meetings, celebrations) to create a culture of constant motivation. 
    5. Knows when to back off. Doesn't overdo it.
4. Grows People: Helps Them Become Better Versions of Themselves 
    1. Obsessed with growing the people under & around them. Has a map in their head of where their people are, and where they want to be, and possibilities they might not see yet. Contantly pushes folks towards their potential, gently. Runs a 'background process' that is always updating this map. 
    2. Identifies superstrengths that should be amplified -- and the weaknesses that must be mitigated to prevent failure. Values true diversity of thought, background, and experience. They know that you must truly value someone in order to grow them.
    3. Gives timely, specific feedback and creates opportunities to practice.  Iterates on the feedbach mechanism based on observed reactions. They use all the tools at their disposal: analogies, books, courses, 1:1s, etc.
    4. Deeply understands Situational Leadership: directing, coaching, supporting, and delegating. Can flex between the different modes based ont he maturity of the employee.
    5. Can distinguish the "rockstars" from the "superstars" -- and the B-players. Invests wisely where their effort can create the most growth -- and winning -- for the team as a whole.
5. Builds Alliances: Wins with the Help of Others
    1. Other managers: builds strong relationships with peers and cross-functional partners. Knows when to ask for help, and when to help in return. Aligns their goals with partner team goals regularly.
    2. Their boss: continously builds alignment on team priorities, problems, and opportunities. Balances taking direction with educating upwards. Builds trust through honest communication, sharing bad news early, and focusing on overall organizational success instead of building a fiefdom.
    3. CEO/execs (if needed): can represent the needs & challenges the team is facing, as well as the opportunities for success and the path to get there. Builds trust that resources will not be wasted through good planning and the right level of communication, at the right time.
    4. Never political: doesn't build alliances for their own sake, or to move their personal agenda forward. Speaks the same message across different forums / different audiences.
    5. Doesn't overdo it: doesn't waste time on 'consensus' when that time can be spent winning or increasing the capacity to win better elsewhere. Knows whose input and commitment are necessary, gets them, and moves forward without seeking universal agreement.
6. Delegate: Multiplies Impact Through Others
    1. Delegates missions, not just tasks. Makes the desired outcome, constraints, and decision authority clear. Gives people ownership of how to get there; aligns responsibility with the ability to affect the result.
    2. Finds the right owner. Understands who has the skill, will, and capacity for the responsibility. Matches the challenge and support to the individual instead of giving everything to whoever is available.
    3. Checks for understanding and progress. Doesn’t mistake agreement for understanding or activity for results. Asks people to explain their approach and checks reality against expectations without micromanaging.
    4. Stays accountable after handing off. Sets clear expectations for when work should finish and when they will intervene. Changes the support—or gets hands-on—when progress demands it, before a recoverable problem becomes a missed outcome.
    5. Builds people who can replace them. Deliberately develops owners and leaders who can run increasingly important responsibilities independently. Uses the time freed up to tackle the next problem; the team keeps moving when they are away.
