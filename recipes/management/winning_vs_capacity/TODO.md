# TODO: future iterations of the talk

Proposals from an audit of the 20 buckets against this Cookbook and *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (Horowitz), *High Output Management* (Grove), *Team Geek* (Fitzpatrick & Collins-Sussman), *The Manager's Path* (Fournier), *An Elegant Puzzle* (Larson), *Radical Candor* (Scott), *The Effective Executive* (Drucker), *Peopleware* (DeMarco & Lister), *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team* (Lencioni), *Accelerate* (Forsgren et al.), *Turn the Ship Around* (Marquet) and *First, Break All the Rules* (Buckingham & Coffman).

**Verdict:** the win vs. capacity split holds up. "One week = one turn" gives a clean test: if it pays off inside this turn, it's winning; otherwise it's capacity. That settles most "which half?" arguments, including the Cookbook's open ones about process, postmortems and budget. The two halves are uneven, though. Winning is all execution with no feedback loop, and capacity leans on the people lifecycle, with no home for trust, the manager's own capacity, retention or quality.

## Top three changes

- [ ] **Make Steer the control loop.** Steer = prioritize *and* track: R/Y/G status, one weekly number, "why did we miss?", and checking in on what you delegated. This fills the biggest gap without adding a bucket. Material: [sprint reports](../winning/reporting/sprint_reports.md), the [data-driven team](../../../meals/data_driven_team.md), sprint metrics in [the Doxel sprint process](../winning/process/examples/doxel_sprint_process.md), Grove's indicators, and the DORA metrics.
- [ ] **End the talk with Trust, as the frame across both halves, not a 21st bucket.** Trust is what every bucket spends or earns: Decide and Motivate (don't oversell), Fund (bad news early), Ally (built before the crisis), Delegate, Cultivate (HRT, psychological safety). Use the Five Dysfunctions pyramid or the inverse trust pyramid from [tools/trust.md](../../../tools/trust.md).
- [ ] **Merge Fund into Ally, and use the free slot for "Sharpen": the manager's own capacity.** Covers calendar (20/40/40), energy, a coach and personal goals ([example personal goals](../increasing_the_capacity_to_win/growing/self/example_personal_goals.md)), plus Drucker's "know thy time" and Horowitz's "The Struggle". This also answers the "OMG, how do I do all that?" slide.

## Other proposed changes

- [ ] **Rename Rally to "Surge" and move overtime into it.** Overtime isn't motivation; like a rally, it borrows from next week (*Peopleware*: overtime is followed by "undertime"). Motivate stays purely about meaning.
- [ ] **Rename Reward to "Reward & Keep".** Add retention: skip-levels, fixing underpay before the outside offer, spotting flight risk, and Q12's "someone at work cares about me". Hiring is slow capacity, and losing people is the fastest way to lose it.
- [ ] **Make quality and operational health explicit** under Automate or Organize ("roads that don't wash out"): on-call health, postmortems, the [engineering maturity model](../../leadership/alignment/growing_up.md), and DORA. If an 11th bucket is OK, call it "Maintain" and use the parked "code needs care" slide.
- [ ] **Link Grow back to Aim:** expectations, scorecards and ladders are "Aim for one person".
- [ ] **Add a "which 2-3 buckets this week?" slide before the close**, using Larson's four team states (falling behind, treading water, repaying debt, innovating), each with its prescription.
- [ ] **Name the IOU wrap-up "management debt"** (Horowitz), with overtime, Surge and cut corners as the examples.
- [ ] **Present Scout and Decide as a pair:** buy information, or act without it.
- [ ] **Add Grove's "full support" to Decide:** free discussion, a clear decision, then everyone commits. This also touches the missing conflict and accountability gap.
- [ ] **Sharpen Delegate vs. Grow.** Both sit on Grove's task-relevant-maturity scale: Grow makes a person better, Delegate frees the manager. The current one-liner ("grow leaders") blurs them.
- [ ] **Treat poisonous people as culture defence** (*Team Geek*): filter at Hire, defend in Cultivate, remove in Prune.

With these changes, each half keeps 10 buckets:

- **Winning:** Aim, Trim, Decide, Scout, Assign, Steer (incl. tracking), Clear & Protect, Motivate, Surge, Carry.
- **Capacity:** Hire, Grow, Delegate, Reward & Keep, Prune, Organize, Automate, Cultivate, Ally, Sharpen.

## Gaps, ranked

1. **Measuring and course-correcting.** All ten winning buckets are open-loop (see Steer above).
2. **Trust and information flow, up, down and sideways.** Covers Grove's information gathering, Horowitz's "minimize politics", [opening the kimono](../../../tools/opening_the_kimono.md), and [information gathering](../winning/info_gathering/README.md).
3. **The manager's own capacity** (see Sharpen above).
4. **Quality and operational health.**
5. **Retention** (see Reward & Keep above).
6. **Conflict, commitment and accountability.** Covered by *Five Dysfunctions*, *Radical Candor*'s Get Stuff Done wheel and Grove's "full support". This is also the parked "Commitment / work hard together" idea.
7. **Setting goals and planning.** Aim assumes B is already chosen. Grove's planning, OKRs and phase gates have no home.

## Overlaps to watch

- **Aim, Trim and Steer** are three cuts of "what to do": which B, how much of it, in what order. Sharpen their one-liners so the difference is obvious.
- **Ally and Fund:** Fund is managing up to one particular ally (merge, as above).
- **Second axes worth one slide:** Grove's leverage (affects many people, lasts long, uses unique knowledge) ranks activities *within* each half. Horowitz's wartime vs. peacetime sets the *mix* between the halves.
- **Recursion:** one level up, your capacity work is your boss's win. Acknowledge it in one line.
